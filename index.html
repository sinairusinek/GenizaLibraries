    How entries were classed

    Every class here is a suggestion made from general knowledge of medieval Hebrew and Judaeo-Arabic literature. Nothing was checked against the book lists themselves.

    Procedure

    • Each index line was read whole: headword, Hebrew gloss, attributed author, the notes inside the references, and any cross-reference.
    • The 3,365 lines were classed in six batches under one written set of conventions, then merged and harmonised.
    • Two decisions per line: what kind of thing it is, and, for works, which FGP category fits.

    Type of entry

    work
    A title, a genre, or a text: a biblical book, a tractate, "commentary on X", "responsa". Always classed.
    author
    A name that stands for an author with no specific work. Classed only when the author's output falls in one field (Galen, Hayyuj); left blank for authors who span several (Saadia, Maimonides, Ibn Ghiyyat).
    person
    Owner, buyer, seller, scribe, official, relative, biblical figure. Not classed.
    object/term
    Binding, quire, volume, copy, paper, script, Torah ornaments, measures. Not classed.
    place/group
    Not classed.
    other
    Unclear lines, and three wrapped lines that continue the entry above them.

    Choosing the category

    • The deepest category that can be justified is used, never a parent when a child fits.
    • When the sub-class is simply unknown, the entry goes to that level's "not defined" residue (לא הוגדר), as FGP itself does.
    • A parent with no sub-field is used only when one entry spans several children, for example a tractate whose references mix Mishnah, Bavli and Yerushalmi copies.
    • תחום לא נקבע is kept for works whose subject is opaque (58 entries).
    • Non-Jewish scientific and philosophical works are classed by subject, not as דתות אחרות. That field holds only religious texts such as the Quran.

    Confidence

    H
    Title or author well known and the class unambiguous (1,463).
    M
    Probable (812).
    L
    A guess: unidentified title, or two fields equally defensible (332).

    Conventions by field

    • Bible. A book or parasha name alone, codices and targum are מקרא ותרגומים. A תפסיר, שרח or פירוש of a biblical book is פרשנות מקרא. Saadia's Tafsir on a book is commentary at confidence M, since translation and commentary travel together.
    • Rabbinic literature. A tractate name alone has no sub-field, unless a note says Mishnah or Talmud. Classical midrash (Rabbah, Tanhuma, Mekhilta, Sifra) is מדרש; later midrash, sermons and homilies are מדרש מאוחר ודרשות.
    • Commentary. On a tractate: פירושי תלמוד בבלי. On the Mishnah: פירושי משנה.
    • Halakha by author. Saadia; Shmuel ben Hofni; Alfasi and his commentators; other Geonim and geonic codes (Hai, Sherira, Halakhot Gedolot, Halakhot Pesukot, She'iltot); Mishneh Torah; Rishonim (Ibn Ghiyyat, Hananel, Nissim); Karaite law. Unknown authorship goes to the residue.
    • Commandments and responsa. Books of commandments are ספרי מצות whoever wrote them. Responsa are split Geonim, Rishonim, Karaite, unknown. Introductions to the Talmud and works of method are מבואות לתלמוד.
    • Thought. Kalam (Saadia's Amanat, al-Muqammis, Yusuf al-Basir); philosophy, Aristotelian or Neoplatonic where known; logic; ethics; Sufi; theology.
    • Sciences and occult. Medicine, pharmacology, astronomy, mathematics and the calendar are מדעים. Astrology, magic, dream interpretation and geomancy are חכמת הנסתר.
    • Language. Grammar, lexicography, masorah, vocalisation, and all glossaries (אלפאט') whatever text they gloss.
    • Liturgy and poetry. Prayer books and the Haggadah are תפילה וברכות; piyyut, selihot, kinot and azharot are פיוט; diwans are שירת חול; tales, maqamat and adab are סיפורת וספרות יפה.
    • Other. Polemics; chronicles and geography; Sefer Yetzira and its commentaries under קבלה; letters and lists as genres under מסמכים.

    Cross-references

    • A "see" entry is classed as what its own headword denotes.
    • When a pure "see" entry points to exactly one work and the two classes differed, the target's class was taken (32 entries). The note column records the earlier class.

    Harmonised after merging

    • Glossaries moved to חכמת הלשון (33 entries).
    • Books of commandments by Saadia and Shmuel ben Hofni moved from halakha to ספרי מצות (2 entries).
    • One tractate (גיטין) aligned with the tractate rule.

    Known inconsistencies

    • Ibn Ghiyyat's Siraj is halakha under its title but tractate commentary under פירוש headwords.
    • Polemical halakha (the fat-tail, the Sabbath lamp, Rabbanite and Karaite disputes) is split between פולמוס and הלכה on thin evidence.
    • Prosody and poetics sit partly in חכמת הלשון and partly in שירת חול.
    • מעמד is classed as piyyut; prayer is defensible.
    • Other works of Shmuel ben Hofni that are theological rather than legal vary between kalam and his halakha category.
    • There is no FGP slot for commentary on midrash; those entries are low confidence.
